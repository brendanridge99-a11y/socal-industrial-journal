Starbucks Announces Third Round of U.S. Layoffs, 300 Corporate Employees Cut

Starbucks announced it will lay off 300 U.S. corporate employees in its third round of job cuts as part of its ongoing turnaround strategy under CEO Brian Niccol. The coffee giant is also closing some regional support offices, with combined severance costs and office space reassessment expected to result in $400 million in restructuring charges. Starbucks expects to record $280 million in noncash charges tied to asset impairments and $120 million in cash charges related to the layoffs. This follows an earlier round that cut 1,100 jobs and another that eliminated 900 nonretail positions as part of a $1 billion restructuring plan.

Starbucks Plans First Corporate Tech Office in India to Cut Costs

Starbucks Corp. announced plans to open a technology office in India as part of a broader initiative to cut $2 billion in costs. The new hub is slated for the company's fiscal year 2027, which begins in October, according to an internal message seen by Bloomberg News. The India location will be Starbucks' first corporate office in the country, and the company expects to begin recruiting once a site is selected later this year.

NextEra Energy Is Said to Be in Talks to Acquire Dominion

NextEra Energy Inc. is in discussions to acquire Dominion Energy Inc. in a mostly stock deal, according to a person familiar with the matter. A transaction could be announced as soon as Monday, though no agreement has been reached and talks could still end without one. The combined company would have an enterprise value of approximately $400 billion including debt. The deal is driven by surging electricity demand from AI data centers, industrial reshoring, and electrification, and would expand NextEra's reach into the PJM Interconnection grid, which extends from Washington to Chicago and encompasses Northern Virginia's data center cluster.

Amazon Rolls Out 30-Minute Delivery on Thousands of Groceries and Essentials

Amazon launched a 30-minute delivery service covering thousands of grocery and everyday essentials items, expanding its ultra-fast fulfillment capabilities to a broader product set beyond its existing same-day offerings. The service relies on Amazon's network of urban micro-fulfillment nodes and delivery infrastructure. The rollout follows Amazon's May 4 launch of Amazon Supply Chain Services (ASCS), which opened its full logistics network to third-party businesses. The 30-minute delivery expansion intensifies pressure on traditional grocery retailers and last-mile delivery providers, and requires continued investment in dense, last-mile warehouse infrastructure.

Boeing Wins 200-Jet Plane Order From China, Trump Says

President Trump announced that China agreed to purchase 200 Boeing commercial jets, marking Boeing's first major aircraft sale to China in nearly a decade, coming out of Trump's summit with President Xi Jinping in Beijing. Trump stated China reserved the right to buy up to 750 aircraft total, though neither the Chinese government nor Boeing issued a confirming statement. Boeing CEO Kelly Ortberg accompanied Trump on the trip; analysts had expected an order of up to 500 aircraft. Boeing shares fell 4.73% on Thursday following the announcement, which fell short of Jefferies' estimate of a 500-plane deal.

Connect CRE · May 15, 2026

Retail Q1 2026: Low Availability and Construction Slow Absorption

Q1 2026 retail market reports from CBRE, JLL, Newmark, and others showed negative net absorption across nearly all tracked reports, driven by a wave of store closures from multiple bankruptcy filings adding available space to the market. Consumer sentiment was negatively affected by lingering inflation, uncertainty about the job market, and the impact of the Iran conflict on fuel costs, per Newmark analysts, though retail sales and consumer spending remained in positive territory. Sun Belt markets outpaced the rest of the country in retail metrics driven by population growth, and JLL noted that for the remainder of 2026, performance will depend more on which markets a portfolio is in than on national averages.
