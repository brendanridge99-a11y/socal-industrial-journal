The Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds rate at 3.5%–3.75% at its April 29 meeting, the third consecutive hold, in an 8-4 vote — the most dissent since October 1992. The Fed acknowledged that Middle East developments have increased uncertainty around the economic outlook and flagged elevated upside risks to inflation. Fed Chair Jerome Powell's term expires May 15, with Kevin Warsh confirmed as his successor. Markets currently price in no changes to rates through year-end 2026, though interest-rate swaps now reflect a 70% probability of a rate hike by April 2027 following the hot April CPI print.
