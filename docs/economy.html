Hormuz Closure Threatens Recession Rivaling 2008, Rapidan Says

A closure of the Strait of Hormuz through August raises the risk of an economic downturn approaching the scale of the 2008 Great Recession, according to Rapidan Energy Group analysis reported by Bloomberg. Rapidan's base case assumes the waterway reopens in July, resulting in an average oil demand reduction of 2.6 million barrels a day and benchmark Brent crude peaking near $130 a barrel over the summer. A more pessimistic scenario extending the closure through August would cause the oil supply deficit to grow to 6 billion barrels per day in Q3. The strait has been largely closed to non-Iranian shipping since the US and Israel launched strikes against Iran in late February.
