US Consumer Sentiment Rises in June as Gasoline Prices Ease

The University of Michigan's preliminary consumer sentiment index rose to 48.9 in June from a record low 44.8 in May, its first increase in four months, as lower gasoline prices provided partial relief from a surge in inflation driven by the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruption. The reading exceeded most economists' expectations in a Bloomberg survey but remains the second-lowest in data going back to the 1970s. Year-ahead inflation expectations fell to 4.6% from 4.8% in May, though still well above the 3.4% reading in February before the Middle East conflict began. The improvement was broad-based across age, education, and political affiliation, with lower-income consumers posting the largest gains due to gasoline's outsized share of their budgets.

The Producer Price Index for final demand rose 1.1% in May 2026, beating the Dow Jones consensus forecast of 0.7%, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Despite the uptick, sentiment remains 13% below January 2026 and 19% below a year ago. Year-ahead inflation expectations edged down from 4.8% in May to 4.6%, while long-run expectations fell from 3.9% to 3.4% — still notably above the 2.8%–3.2% range seen throughout 2024.
