US Inflation Picks Up to Three-Year High, Eroding Paychecks

The U.S. Consumer Price Index rose 0.5% on a seasonally adjusted basis in May and 4.2% year-over-year — the highest annual rate since April 2023 — according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data released June 10 and covered in Bloomberg's June 12 analysis. Energy prices drove the bulk of the increase, jumping 3.9% for the month and 23.5% over 12 months, attributable to the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict's impact on oil markets. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, rose 0.2% month-over-month and 2.9% year-over-year. Transportation services rose 4.1% annually, a key cost driver for logistics and supply chain operators.

ECB Stands Out With First G7 Rate Hike Since Start of Iran War

The European Central Bank raised its deposit facility rate 25 basis points to 2.25% on June 11, its first rate increase in nearly three years, as the Iran war drives energy prices and inflation above target. The ECB revised its 2026 eurozone headline inflation forecast to 3.0% while cutting its GDP growth outlook to 0.8% for the year, down from a prior estimate of 0.9%. ECB President Christine Lagarde stated the war is 'weighing on activity' and that elevated energy prices will keep inflation above the 2% target into the first half of 2027.
