Inland Empire Industrial Slump Deepens, But Brokers Say Rebound May Be Near

The Inland Empire's industrial vacancy rate reached 7.8% in Q1 2026 with negative net absorption of 4.7 million SF, driven by four 1 million+ SF move-outs as tenants consolidated or relocated, per CBRE. However, the construction pipeline collapsed to just 102,000 SF delivered in Q1 2026, down sharply from nearly 2 million SF in Q1 2025, leading brokers to forecast that vacancy has likely peaked or is near its peak. Colliers tracked IE West rents at $1.10/SF and IE East at $0.90/SF, with a lack of consensus on whether rents have further to fall complicating transaction pricing. For IE brokers, the critical takeaway is that the supply-side problem is essentially resolved — the risk of further vacancy increases from new deliveries is minimal, setting the stage for gradual market recovery in late 2026 or 2027.

Ships at LA's Ports Face a Fuel Shock That's Shaking the Economy

Cargo ships at the Ports of LA and Long Beach are facing fuel costs nearly 20% higher than other major global ports, as the Strait of Hormuz closure since late February has cut off California's average 200,000 barrels per day of Persian Gulf oil imports. The average price of very low-sulfur fuel oil has surged 70% to $925 per metric ton globally since the Iran War started, with fuel now representing 25% of total voyage costs from Asia to Los Angeles. Shipping majors including Hapag-Lloyd, Maersk, and Amazon are passing costs onto shippers, consumers, and importers via surcharges. For IE industrial brokers and tenants, rising freight costs are reducing the volume and profitability of goods flowing through SoCal warehouses, potentially softening warehouse demand from import-dependent distributors and retailers.
