The Bottom Line

The defining tension in Southern California industrial real estate right now is the collision between structurally elevated costs and stubbornly high vacancy: West Coast port-adjacent availability has crossed 13% as China's share of U.S. imports has collapsed from 22% to 7.5%, yet the capital — institutional and otherwise — keeps flowing in, evidenced by Blackstone's $4 billion CMBS refinancing, Goodman's 1.6 million-square-foot Rancho Cucamonga lease-up, and a nine-million-plus-square-foot entitlement in Palmdale. Layered on top is a macro environment that has effectively killed any near-term rate relief, with April CPI at 3.8% and PPI at 6.0% driven by an energy shock that is also pushing West region diesel to $6.60 a gallon and freight costs up more than 20% on flat volumes. The Trump-Xi summit may have extended the trade truce and put a tentative floor under tariffs, but with average duties on Chinese goods still at 47.5% and supply chains already structurally rewired toward Southeast Asia and India, the demand profile that built this market is not coming back in its prior form — and brokers working large-format logistics space need to understand that the leasing story here is now about replacement demand, not recovery.

◈ Economics 39

Inflation surged to 3.8% CPI and 6.0% PPI year-over-year in April — both well above consensus — driven primarily by an energy shock tied to the ongoing U.S.-Iran war and near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with the Cleveland Fed now projecting May CPI at 4.2% and the Survey of Professional Forecasters calling for 6% inflation in Q2. Markets have effectively priced out any Fed rate cuts through 2026, with a 39% chance of a hike now priced in by year-end, 10-year Treasury yields at their highest since July 2025, and 30-year yields briefly topping 5%. Kevin Warsh was confirmed as Fed Chair in a 54-45 Senate vote on May 13, succeeding Jerome Powell whose term expired May 15, with his first rate decision set for the June 16-17 FOMC meeting.

◉ Industries 32

National industrial vacancy sits in the mid-7% range entering Q2 2026 and is projected to edge higher into early 2027, with West Coast port markets — directly relevant to Southern California brokers — seeing availability in large, newer logistics properties exceed 13%, compared to roughly 9% for East Coast peers. Despite the soft headline numbers, Q1 2026 leasing activity surged 17.8% year-over-year to 145.2 million square feet nationally, and First Industrial reported a 41% cash rental rate increase on 2026 leases with a major Inland Empire renewal among its highlights. On the capital markets side, Blackstone locked in more than $4 billion in new industrial CMBS refinancings across 213 properties, with in-place rents trailing market rents by roughly $2.75 per square foot — underscoring the rent-growth runway that continues to attract institutional capital to the sector even as lending sentiment has cooled.

◎ Tech 4

AI infrastructure investment is reshaping industrial real estate demand on multiple fronts: Cushman & Wakefield projects AI adoption will add roughly 330 million square feet of U.S. commercial space over the next decade, and CBRE data shows tech and data center occupiers captured the largest share of manufacturing leasing in Q1 2026, with data center supply chain tenants — prefab contractors, component manufacturers, and maintenance operators — emerging as a distinct new driver of industrial absorption. The power competition between data centers and industrial users is intensifying, with data centers projected to reach 12% of national electricity consumption by 2028, up from 4% in 2023. Cerebras Systems' Nasdaq debut — shares opened up 89%, pushing fully diluted valuation above $100 billion on $5.55 billion raised — signals that investor appetite for AI infrastructure remains at peak levels heading into potential larger IPOs from SpaceX and OpenAI later this year.

◐ Politics 13

The Trump-Xi summit concluded May 15 in Beijing with China agreeing to purchase U.S. oil and 200 Boeing aircraft, and both sides extending the one-year trade truce set in October 2025 — though average U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods remain at 47.5% and no major structural trade agreements were reached. The two leaders established a three-year "strategic stability" framework and agreed to a managed trade mechanism targeting approximately $30 billion in tariff reductions on each side, with China expected to commit to "double-digit billion" in annual U.S. agricultural purchases over three years. Separately, universal U.S. tariffs remain at 10% through July 24 with possible increases to 15%, Vietnam has emerged as a significant new tariff target due to its large U.S. trade surplus, and China's share of U.S. goods imports has dropped from 22% in 2017 to just 7.5% in early 2026 as global supply chains continue to restructure.

◍ Supply Chain & Logistics 9

Southern California's industrial market is anchored by a record-breaking Port of Long Beach, which processed 9.9 million TEUs in 2025 and has committed $3.2 billion — the largest capital plan of any North American port — to triple on-dock rail capacity and cut ship-to-train transfer time from four days to 24 hours by 2032. On the cost side, West region diesel hit $6.60 per gallon in March, driving freight spending up 20.5% year-over-year despite flat volumes, with Hormuz-driven energy market instability keeping fuel costs volatile and unpredictable. Meanwhile, Amazon's launch of Amazon Supply Chain Services — opening its 13-billion-item-per-year logistics network to all businesses at 96.4% on-time delivery — is entering direct competition with traditional 3PLs, while the Trump-Xi summit signals a potential floor on U.S.-China tariffs, though China's share of U.S. trade has already fallen from 13% to 6.4% as sourcing patterns have structurally shifted toward Southeast Asia and India.

◑ Local Deals & Developments 5

The Southern California industrial market faces mounting headwinds as West Coast port-adjacent vacancy exceeds 13% — well above East Coast peers — driven by China's declining share of U.S. imports, now just 7.5%, down from 22% in 2017. Against that backdrop, two of the largest industrial development stories in the region are moving forward: Goodman launched leasing at a 1.6 million-square-foot logistics campus in Rancho Cucamonga, while Covington Group secured full entitlements for a 9.4 million-square-foot Antelope Valley Commerce Center in Palmdale — described as one of the largest industrial entitlements ever completed in Los Angeles County. On the personnel front, Rexford Industrial's new CEO Laura Clark signaled a strategic shift toward infill asset ownership over new development, and Transwestern Development hired former Alere Property Group CIO Clark Neuhoff to lead its West Coast industrial development efforts out of Newport Beach.
