10 stories from the past 72 hours

Industrial real estate activity over the past 72 hours reflects a bifurcated market: institutional capital remains aggressive—Blackstone's €2.3B French portfolio acquisition, BGO/Greenlaw's Georgia warehouse buy, and Redfearn Capital's $60M Florida deals signal sustained demand—while new development starts are down 25% versus historical averages. Small-bay and infill product continues outperforming, with sub-5% vacancy. Office-to-industrial conversions are emerging as a notable supply strategy, exemplified by Foundry Commercial's 1.9M-square-foot Dallas-Fort Worth redevelopment pipeline.

Blackstone's Data Center REIT Raises $1.75 Billion in IPO to Buy Data Centers

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (BXDC) completed its US IPO on May 14 on the NYSE, pricing 87.5 million shares at $20 each to raise $1.75 billion, with underwriters holding a 30-day option to lift total proceeds to $2 billion. The REIT will focus on acquiring newly constructed, stabilized data centers valued between $250 million and $1.5 billion, occupied by investment-grade hyperscale tenants on long-term leases, targeting yields of 5.75% to 7%. Blackstone has identified approximately $25 billion in potential near-term data center purchases in Northern Virginia, Ohio, Phoenix, Maryland, and Austin. Nick Pell, former president and CIO of Blackstone's Link Logistics industrial REIT arm, was appointed CEO of the new entity.

Small Bay Now Dominates Industrial and It Will Stay That Way

GlobeSt published an analysis on May 14, 2026 examining how small-bay industrial product has become the dominant force in the industrial market. HALO businesses and the evolution of the broader economy were cited as major demand drivers behind the shift. Small-bay and micro-warehouse assets in last-mile and infill locations continue to post vacancy rates below 5%, making them among the most competitive subsectors in the market. The analysis concludes that the structural demand for small-bay industrial is durable and unlikely to reverse.
