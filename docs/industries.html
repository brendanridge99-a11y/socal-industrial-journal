Blackstone's BXDC Data Center REIT Raises $1.75B in NYSE IPO

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (BXDC) completed its NYSE IPO on May 14, raising $1.75 billion by selling 87.5 million shares at $20 per share, with underwriters holding a 30-day option to increase total proceeds to $2 billion. The REIT, structured as a blind pool with no assets yet acquired, will target newly constructed, stabilized data centers valued between $250 million and $1.5 billion, occupied by investment-grade hyperscale tenants on long-term leases at yields of 5.75% to 7%. Blackstone has identified approximately $25 billion in potential near-term acquisitions across Northern Virginia, Ohio, Phoenix, Maryland, and Austin. Nick Pell, former president and CIO of Blackstone's Link Logistics industrial REIT, was named CEO of the new entity.

Retail Q1 2026: Low Availability and Construction Slow Absorption

Connect CRE · May 15, 2026

Connect CRE: AI and CRE — Industry Moving Cautiously, Prioritizing Explainability and Auditability

A joint study by First American Data and DealGround found that AI adoption in commercial real estate is proceeding cautiously, with firms prioritizing explainability, provenance, and defensible outputs over full automation. The study noted that many CRE workflows depend on fragmented historical data, unstructured documents, and legacy systems where small inaccuracies can create significant operational or financial consequences. AI and automation were identified as the leading catalysts for change in corporate real estate portfolio management, but actual implementation remains a work in progress. The study concluded that the market is 'pressure-testing whether current AI systems can consistently produce reliable, auditable outputs.'

Small Bay Now Dominates Industrial and It Will Stay That Way

GlobeSt published an analysis on May 14, 2026 examining how small-bay industrial product has become the dominant force in the industrial market. HALO businesses and the evolution of the broader economy were cited as major demand drivers behind the shift. Small-bay and micro-warehouse assets in last-mile and infill locations continue to post vacancy rates below 5%, making them among the most competitive subsectors in the market. The analysis concludes that the structural demand for small-bay industrial is durable and unlikely to reverse.
