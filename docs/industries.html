Amazon Expands LTL Freight Service to All U.S. Businesses, Sparking Freight Stock Sell-Off

Amazon on June 10 opened its less-than-truckload shipping service to all U.S. businesses — not just sellers shipping into Amazon fulfillment centers — delivering to third-party warehouses, distribution centers, and retail partners nationwide as part of its Amazon Supply Chain Services program. The announcement triggered immediate market reactions: Old Dominion fell ~5%, FedEx Freight (newly spun off June 1) fell ~7%, Saia fell ~3.3%, and ArcBest dropped ~4%. Amazon operates more than 80,000 trailers and 24,000 intermodal containers, though analysts at TD Cowen estimate fewer than 100 cross-dock terminals, well below the ~300 average for top LTL incumbents. Procter & Gamble, 3M, Lands' End, and American Eagle Outfitters were named as early customers of the expanded service.

West Marine Confirms 59-Store Closure List in Chapter 11 Bankruptcy Across 23 States

West Marine Inc., the largest U.S. boating and marine supplies retailer, confirmed the closure of 59 stores across 23 states under its Chapter 11 restructuring following a May 17 bankruptcy filing in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware. Court documents filed June 1 identify every location, landlord, and address, and the company disclosed it continues to analyze its store portfolio and may file additional closure lists. The consulting agreement with Hilco was written assuming approximately 95 total store closures. West Marine operates more than 230 locations across 38 states and Puerto Rico and carries roughly $800 million in debt from a 2017 leveraged buyout controlled by Oaktree Capital Management and L Catterton.

Amazon Expands LTL Freight Service Nationwide to All Businesses and Destinations, Leveraging 80,000-Trailer Network

Amazon has expanded its less-than-truckload freight service beyond inbound-to-Amazon shipments to include any destination nationwide — including third-party warehouses, distribution centers, and retail partners. The expanded LTL offering is part of Amazon Supply Chain Services and leverages the company's logistics network of 80,000 trailers and 24,000 intermodal containers. The service supports palletized freight ranging from one to six pallets, or between 150 and 15,000 pounds, using shared trailer capacity. Features include real-time GPS tracking, drop trailer options, and EDI integrations.
