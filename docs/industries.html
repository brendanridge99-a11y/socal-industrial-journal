Sleep Number Files Bankruptcy to Sell Itself, Blames Tariffs

Mattress maker Sleep Number Corp. filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on June 12 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, with an agreement to sell the company to Sleep Country Canada Inc. The company cited 'the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain' as a contributing cause of the filing. Even after the U.S. Supreme Court struck down certain Trump IEEPA tariffs, Sleep Number stated the broader trade landscape remained complex with ongoing regulatory uncertainties. The bankruptcy follows years of weak demand and mounting financial pressure, including a $25 million term loan added in April 2026 ahead of the filing.

West Marine Confirms 59-Store Closure List in Chapter 11 Bankruptcy Across 23 States

West Marine Inc., the largest U.S. boating and marine supplies retailer, confirmed the closure of 59 stores across 23 states under its Chapter 11 restructuring following a May 17 bankruptcy filing in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware. Court documents filed June 1 identify every location, landlord, and address, and the company disclosed it continues to analyze its store portfolio and may file additional closure lists. The consulting agreement with Hilco was written assuming approximately 95 total store closures. West Marine operates more than 230 locations across 38 states and Puerto Rico and carries roughly $800 million in debt from a 2017 leveraged buyout controlled by Oaktree Capital Management and L Catterton.

Retail Sales Rise in May 2026 as Consumer Spending Holds Strong

Total retail sales excluding automobile dealers and gasoline stations rose 0.42% month-over-month and 7.19% year-over-year in May, according to the CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor powered by Affinity Solutions. Retail sales increased for the eighth consecutive month despite high gas prices and ongoing inflation. NRF president and CEO Matthew Shay attributed the gains to a resilient labor market and consumer willingness to spend, while noting that fading support from large tax refunds is pushing consumers to prioritize essentials. Core retail sales (also excluding restaurants) rose 0.39% month-over-month and 6.98% year-over-year.
