Saudi Wealth Fund Weighs Logistics Merger to Attract Global Investment

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund is considering consolidating its transport and supply-chain assets — including ports, rail, and shipping — into a single logistics entity to attract foreign investment and better serve the kingdom's trade hubs amid the Iran war disruption. The PIF has held early-stage talks on combining parts of its portfolio including the $8.3 billion National Shipping Company of Saudi Arabia and Saudi Global Ports, according to people familiar with the matter. The enlarged firm could evolve into a vehicle for multibillion-dollar investments and potentially include an IPO to bring in international investors. Discussions gained urgency after the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz exposed vulnerabilities in Middle Eastern supply chains.

Gulf States Tell Ships Not to Use Iran's Strait of Hormuz Route

Five Middle Eastern countries — Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates — formally rejected Iran's establishment of the Persian Gulf Strait Authority to control transit through the Strait of Hormuz. In a letter to the International Maritime Organization distributed earlier this week, the five nations said commercial and merchant vessels should not engage with the PGSA or cross the waterway using Iran's designated route. The joint statement escalates the ongoing standoff over the strategically vital waterway, which has been largely closed to commercial shipping since the US-Israeli military campaign against Iran began on February 28. The letter signals a hardening of Gulf state opposition to Iran's claimed maritime authority.

Survey: Port leaders call for $6.7 billion cargo equipment spending

A survey of 25 senior port and terminal executives conducted by the National Association of Waterfront Employers found that U.S. ports require $6.7 billion in cargo equipment investment over the next five years to maintain efficiency and global competitiveness. Port leaders identified aging equipment as a key challenge to operational performance. The survey did not specify how the investment total was distributed across individual ports or equipment categories.
