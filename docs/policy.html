China Commits $17B Annually in U.S. Farm Goods, 200 Boeing Jets After Trump-Xi Summit

China agreed to purchase at least $17 billion per year in U.S. agricultural products for 2026, 2027, and 2028, building on a prior commitment to buy 25 million metric tons of soybeans annually, bringing total annual agricultural commitments to roughly $27 billion. China approved an initial order of 200 American-made Boeing aircraft — the first Chinese commitment to Boeing since 2017 — though Boeing shares fell 3.8% as the figure was smaller than pre-summit investor expectations. The two countries established a U.S.-China Board of Trade and a U.S.-China Board of Investment, with the trade board potentially facilitating tariff reductions on some $30 billion in non-sensitive goods. China also agreed to restore market access for more than 400 U.S. beef facilities, resume poultry imports from USDA-certified bird-flu-free states, and address U.S. concerns over supply chain shortages of rare earths including yttrium, scandium, neodymium, and indium.

LIRR Strike Enters Fourth Day as 250,000 Commuters Face Disruptions

The Long Island Rail Road strike extended into its fourth day Monday, marking the first work stoppage on the nation's busiest commuter railroad in over 30 years. Roughly 3,500 engineers, signalmen and electrical workers walked off the job Saturday after negotiations between the Metropolitan Transportation Authority and a five-union coalition collapsed over wages and healthcare premiums. The shutdown has affected more than 250,000 daily commuters, straining roads and alternative transit options across the region during the first weekday of the strike. The National Mediation Board summoned parties to negotiations in Manhattan, where talks continued slowly into Monday evening.

BUILD America 250 Act Sets AV Framework, Revises Broker and Safety Rules

The BUILD America 250 Act establishes the first federal regulatory framework for autonomous commercial trucks, requiring the Department of Transportation to develop safety standards within two years, while stopping short of authorizing immediate deployment. The legislation also revises freight broker qualification requirements, overhauling the DataQs process for disputing safety violations and setting a federal timeline for hair follicle drug testing of commercial drivers. Liability questions for autonomous vehicles would remain subject to jury determination under the bill. Additional provisions include funding for workforce retraining programs tied to the autonomous trucking transition, with broader implications for broker licensing standards, motor carrier compliance, and insurance renewal cycles.

Perfectus Aluminum and Related Companies Agree to Pay $549.5M to Settle False Claims Act Allegations Relating to Evaded Customs Duties

Perfectus Aluminum Inc. and affiliated companies agreed to pay $549.5 million to settle False Claims Act allegations that they evaded customs duties on aluminum imports, in one of the largest customs fraud settlements in US history. The case involved allegations of tariff evasion through misclassification of aluminum products to avoid anti-dumping and countervailing duties. The settlement signals intensified federal enforcement of customs compliance as part of the broader crackdown on tariff evasion following the Supreme Court's ruling on IEEPA tariffs. Importers, freight brokers, and 3PLs are expected to face heightened customs scrutiny as a result of the case.

Appeals Court Keeps Section 122 Tariffs In Place (For Now): What Importers Need To Know

On May 7, 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade ruled that the Trump Administration's 10% global tariffs imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 were unlawful, issuing a permanent injunction for three named plaintiffs — the State of Washington, Burlap & Barrel, and Basic Fun. On May 12, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit entered an administrative stay, suspending the CIT's ruling while the appeal proceeds, meaning Section 122 tariffs remain in effect for all other importers. Importers paid an estimated $25 billion in Section 122 duties since February 24, 2026. The tariffs are currently set to expire July 24, 2026, but the administration may impose replacement tariffs under Section 301 or Section 232.
