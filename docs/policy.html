LIRR Strike Ends After MTA, Five Unions Reach Tentative Deal

The Long Island Rail Road resumed partial service Tuesday at noon on four branches after the MTA and five unions reached a tentative agreement, ending a three-day strike. Approximately 3,500 engineers, signalmen, and electrical workers had walked off the job Saturday following a wage and healthcare premium dispute, marking the first LIRR work stoppage since a two-day strike in 1994. New York Governor Kathy Hochul announced the tentative deal late Monday after the National Mediation Board summoned parties to negotiations in Manhattan. The agreement on wage increases still requires ratification by rank-and-file union members, with the MTA expecting full operations to resume by Tuesday evening.

US-China Tariff Rates: Where Things Stand as of May 20, 2026

China and the US appear to have agreed on terms for restoration of US tariffs on Chinese goods through Section 301 investigations, following the Trump-Xi summit in Beijing, though no extension to the one-year trade truce through November 2026 has been finalized. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Reuters on May 19 that he believes China will accept restoration of prior tariff rates 'as long as they don't go higher,' with the two Section 301 investigations set to conclude this summer. The effective tariff rate on many Chinese goods shipped to the US remains close to 30%, still the highest of any country, reflecting a 15% global tariff in effect since February 24, 2026 plus surviving Section 232 and Section 301 duties. Section 301 tariff exclusions for 178 products have been extended until November 10, 2026.
