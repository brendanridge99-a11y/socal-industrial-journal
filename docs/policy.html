USTR Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs of 10%–12.5% Proposed on 60 Trading Partners; Public Comment Deadline July 6

On June 2, 2026, USTR proposed additional duties of 10% or 12.5% on virtually all U.S. imports from 60 countries — representing over 99% of U.S. import volume — following Section 301 investigations into failures to prohibit goods produced with forced labor. The proposed tariffs are designed to replace IEEPA-based duties struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026, ahead of the temporary Section 122 global surcharge's expiration on July 24. California Air Resources Board / California Legislative Analyst's Office· June 13, 2026

CARB Cap-and-Invest Revised Regulations Advance; Industrial Entities Eligible for MDI Allowances Under Updated Program

California Air Resources Board published revised Cap-and-Invest regulations on April 14, 2026, incorporating significant changes aimed at reducing industrial leakage — the risk of businesses leaving California to avoid compliance — and addressing affordability. The revised program expands the Market-Based Directive Incentive allowance pool, with half reserved for refining and similar industries and half for other industrial sectors. A 15-day public comment period closed May 4, 2026, and CARB voted on the regulations May 28. Industrial entities subject to the program must now plan for updated post-2026 allowance allocations and revised GHG-reduction benchmarks.

Dorsey / Gibson Dunn / Thompson Hine / USTR· June 13, 2026

Section 122 Global Tariff Set to Expire July 24; Importers Face Transition Gap Risk as Section 301 Hearings Begin July 7

The Trump administration's Section 122 global 10% import surcharge — issued as a replacement for IEEPA tariffs struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026 — expires July 24, 2026, absent Congressional action. USTR's proposed Section 301 forced-labor duties of 10%–12.5% on 60 countries are not yet in effect, and the public hearing timeline beginning July 7 may not produce finalized tariffs before the Section 122 lapse, creating a potential gap in tariff coverage. Separately, on June 1, 2026, President Trump issued a proclamation adjusting Section 232 duties on aluminum, copper, and steel and their derivative products. Importers are advised to model exposure under both the 10% and 12.5% duty scenarios and prepare comment-record submissions by the July 6 deadline.
