US Refunds $22 Billion in Tariffs After Supreme Court Ruling on Trump Policy

The U.S. Treasury refunded nearly $22 billion in tariff revenue to importers in May, the first tranche of repayments following the Supreme Court's February ruling that struck down the administration's IEEPA-based tariff authority. The refund amount was roughly equal to tariffs collected during the same month, causing net customs duty revenue to turn slightly negative—approximately negative $42 million—for the first time in Bloomberg-compiled Treasury data going back to 2015. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has said tariffs imposed under other legal authorities will replace the lost revenue in 2026, though those measures are not fully in place. The administration has appealed a court order to refund all importers who paid the $166 billion collected under the now-invalidated IEEPA authority.

Trump Allowed to Enforce Section 122 Tariffs Pending Appeals Court Decision

A federal appeals court in Washington ruled June 11 that the Trump administration may continue enforcing its 10% global tariffs under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 while litigation proceeds. The court held the government made a sufficient showing it is likely to prevail in its challenge to a lower court ruling that had invalidated the tariffs. The Court of International Trade had previously struck down the Section 122 tariffs in a 2-1 decision in May, following the Supreme Court's earlier invalidation of IEEPA-based tariffs in February. The Section 122 tariffs, imposed February 24, are set to expire July 24 absent congressional reauthorization.

Section 301 Forced Labor Tariff Investigations Launched Against 60 Countries; New Brazil 25% Tariff Proposed

The USTR issued a Federal Register Notice on March 12, 2026, initiating Section 301 investigations into 60 countries' failure to ban imports of goods produced with forced labor, proposing a 10% or 12.5% tariff on affected products after the current Section 122 global 10% tariff expires July 24, 2026. A separate Section 301 case on Brazil proposes a 25% tariff on most Brazilian products with standard exemptions. The Section 122 10% global tariff remains in effect despite a Court of International Trade ruling it unlawful, as that ruling did not issue a universal injunction and the government has appealed to the Federal Circuit. The new Section 301 tariffs would not stack on top of existing Section 232 metal tariffs.
