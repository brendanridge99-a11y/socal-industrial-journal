DC Circuit Punts on NLRB Power to Remedy Labor Law Violations

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit declined to rule on the National Labor Relations Board's authority to order employers to pay for the economic consequences of their unlawful conduct, in a case involving software company Vermont Information Processing Inc. The court held that the company could not challenge the NLRB's broad remedial powers established in the 2022 Thryv, Inc. decision because it had failed to raise those arguments before the board itself. The appeals court partially upheld the board's underlying ruling against the company, with the decision dividing along party lines.

Section 122 Global Tariff Appeal Proceeds at Federal Circuit; 10% Surcharge Remains in Force Through July 24

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit's administrative stay of the Court of International Trade's May 7 ruling — which invalidated the Trump administration's 10% global Section 122 tariff — remains in place, meaning CBP continues to collect the surcharge from all non-plaintiff importers. The Federal Circuit stayed the CIT's permanent injunction on May 12 while considering a longer-lasting stay pending appeal; on May 20, the CIT separately denied the government's own stay motion, finding no irreparable harm or likelihood of success on the merits. The Section 122 tariffs are scheduled to expire July 24, 2026 absent congressional extension, and USTR's parallel Section 301 investigations covering 76 tariff determinations are expected to conclude before that date. Importers remain obligated to pay the 10% surcharge and should preserve protest and entry documentation to maintain potential refund rights.

How the Top U.S. Trade Chief Sees the Future of US-China Trade

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told the Council on Foreign Relations that the administration will solicit public input on which 'non-strategic' Chinese goods should see lower tariffs following President Trump's recent visit to China. Greer said the administration will also ask which American goods China should buy more of. The USMCA renewal deadline is five weeks away, and Greer offered little optimism about Canada's place in the deal. The administration's approach signals a shift toward targeted tariff reductions rather than broad economic rebalancing demands on China.
