Companies Are Getting Tariff Refunds. Here's What They Plan to Do With the Money.

The US Customs and Border Protection began processing the first wave of electronic tariff refunds on May 12, returning billions of dollars to importers who paid levies later struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026. CBP said about 15% of submitted claims were rejected due to incorrect information or ineligible shipments. Major companies including General Motors said they expect hundreds of millions of dollars in refunds; Citi analysis found Walmart is owed approximately $10 billion, Target $2 billion, and Nike $1 billion. Companies have stated plans to use refunds to pay down debt, rehire workers, restock inventory, or potentially lower prices, while consumers are not directly eligible for refunds.

Perfectus Aluminum and Related Companies Agree to Pay $549.5M to Settle False Claims Act Allegations Relating to Evaded Customs Duties

Perfectus Aluminum Inc. and affiliated companies agreed to pay $549.5 million to settle False Claims Act allegations that they evaded customs duties on aluminum imports, in one of the largest customs fraud settlements in US history. The case involved allegations of tariff evasion through misclassification of aluminum products to avoid anti-dumping and countervailing duties. The settlement signals intensified federal enforcement of customs compliance as part of the broader crackdown on tariff evasion following the Supreme Court's ruling on IEEPA tariffs. Importers, freight brokers, and 3PLs are expected to face heightened customs scrutiny as a result of the case.
