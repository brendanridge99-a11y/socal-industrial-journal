Trump Heads to Beijing With Tariff Uncertainty, AI Communication, and Strait of Hormuz in Focus

President Trump departed for Beijing on May 12 for a summit with President Xi Jinping scheduled for May 14–15, with tariffs, AI policy, and China's role in the Iran conflict as top agenda items. The White House indicated Trump will announce new trade deals, most likely focused on agriculture, energy, and aerospace, with Boeing executives accompanying the trip and new Chinese aircraft purchases expected. The administration also plans to announce a new US-China 'board of trade' and 'board of investment' to improve communication around the AI rivalry. Top CEOs including Elon Musk of Tesla and Tim Cook of Apple are also on the trip.

Bloomberg: Trump Tariffs on Vietnam and Southeast Asia Reshape Global Supply Chains One Year After Liberation Day

One year after 'Liberation Day,' Bloomberg's analysis finds that Trump's tariffs have fueled a shift in global supply chains, though not in the way the administration envisioned. China was targeted with tariffs peaking at 125%, leading to a US-China one-year truce reached in October, while China's overall trade surplus soared to record levels as it pivoted exports to markets outside the US. Universal tariffs officially sit at 10% until July 24, with threats they could rise to 15%, and the White House launched new trade investigations into 16 and 60 economies respectively, including Vietnam — now identified as a bigger tariff target due to its large US trade surplus. Foxconn and other major manufacturers continue expanding capacity in Vietnam's Bac Ninh province.
