Companies Are Getting Tariff Refunds. Here's What They Plan to Do With the Money.

The US Customs and Border Protection began processing the first wave of electronic tariff refunds on May 12, returning billions of dollars to importers who paid levies later struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026. CBP said about 15% of submitted claims were rejected due to incorrect information or ineligible shipments. Major companies including General Motors said they expect hundreds of millions of dollars in refunds; Citi analysis found Walmart is owed approximately $10 billion, Target $2 billion, and Nike $1 billion. Companies have stated plans to use refunds to pay down debt, rehire workers, restock inventory, or potentially lower prices, while consumers are not directly eligible for refunds.

Trump and Xi Agree on Strategic Framework, Boeing Deal After Beijing Summit

President Trump concluded a two-day summit with President Xi Jinping in Beijing on May 15, with China agreeing to purchase U.S. oil and 200 Boeing aircraft. The two leaders established a 'strategic stability' framework to govern the bilateral relationship for three years, and Trump invited Xi to the White House for a visit on September 24. The existing one-year trade truce set in October 2025 remains in place, with average U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods currently at 47.5%, while that status quo is set to expire in November. Analysts noted that major sticking points on trade, semiconductors, and AI remained unresolved despite positive optics from the summit.

Greer: US, China Willing to Continue Trade Truce

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, speaking exclusively to Bloomberg on the sidelines of the Trump-Xi summit in Beijing, signaled cautious optimism that US-China trade relations are stabilizing. US Expects Dramatic Agricultural Purchases by China, Greer Says

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told Bloomberg Television that the U.S. anticipates China will commit to billions in American agricultural purchases as the Trump-Xi summit in Beijing concludes. Greer specifically referenced 'double-digit billion' annual aggregate agricultural purchases — not just soybeans — over a three-year period. The average U.S. tariff on Chinese goods currently stands at 47.5% following the South Korea summit, up from 3.1% before Trump's first term, per the Peterson Institute for International Economics. Two-way goods trade between the U.S. and China totaled approximately $415 billion in 2025, down sharply from a $690 billion peak in 2022.

Wall Street Journal · May 15, 2026 🔒 Subscriber

Perfectus Aluminum and Related Companies Agree to Pay $549.5M to Settle False Claims Act Allegations Relating to Evaded Customs Duties

Perfectus Aluminum Inc. and affiliated companies agreed to pay $549.5 million to settle False Claims Act allegations that they evaded customs duties on aluminum imports, in one of the largest customs fraud settlements in US history. The case involved allegations of tariff evasion through misclassification of aluminum products to avoid anti-dumping and countervailing duties. The settlement signals intensified federal enforcement of customs compliance as part of the broader crackdown on tariff evasion following the Supreme Court's ruling on IEEPA tariffs. Importers, freight brokers, and 3PLs are expected to face heightened customs scrutiny as a result of the case.

House Cuts Build-To-Rent Ban From Latest Bill, Targets Vote Next Week

The House removed a ban on build-to-rent housing from its latest legislative package and is targeting a vote the following week on the modified bill, after President Trump publicly pushed House members to pass a housing bill that had included a forced-sale provision for BTR properties. The real estate lobby had sounded the alarm over the forced-sale provision in the original bill, arguing it would chill investment in single-family rental development. The modified bill's passage timeline remains uncertain pending negotiations among House members.
