AI Chipmaker Cerebras Shares Soar 89% After Year's Biggest IPO

AI chipmaker and data center firm Cerebras Systems raised $5.55 billion in its Nasdaq IPO, priced at $185 per share — above the marketed range — giving it an initial market value of approximately $40 billion. Shares opened at $350, up 89%, pushing the valuation above $100 billion on a fully diluted basis. Demand for the offering exceeded available shares by more than 20 times. The company has partnerships with Amazon Web Services and OpenAI, and the debut is viewed as an early test of investor appetite ahead of potential larger offerings from SpaceX and OpenAI later in 2026.

project44 Launches Autopilot to Cut Freight Costs, Improve Data Quality, Optimize Inventory Levels, and Accelerate Cash Flow

Supply chain visibility platform project44 launched 'Autopilot,' an AI-driven tool designed to autonomously cut freight costs, improve shipment data quality, optimize inventory levels, and accelerate cash flow for shippers and logistics operators. The system uses machine learning to make automated decisions across freight procurement, routing, and inventory positioning without requiring manual intervention from supply chain teams. The launch comes as shippers face elevated freight costs due to oil price spikes from the Strait of Hormuz closure and continued tariff-related supply chain disruptions. project44 positions Autopilot as a response to the growing complexity of operating supply chains in a volatile trade and energy environment.

Proprietary Data Obsession Is Holding Real Estate Back From AI

At Bisnow's New York AI & Technology event held at Silverstein Properties' 7 World Trade Center on May 13, industry executives discussed how real estate firms' reluctance to share proprietary data with AI platforms is limiting adoption of the technology. Fundrise CEO Ben Miller stated that real estate companies are 'afraid to adopt the cutting-edge' AI tools due to concerns about data exposure to large language models. Onyxia Cyber CEO Sivan Tehila warned of 'shadow AI,' where employees use AI tools without authorization, potentially leaking sensitive company data. IBM research cited at the event found that over one-third of employees admit sharing sensitive work information with AI tools without employer permission.

BisNow · May 13, 2026

Texas Might Soon Have More Data Centers, But Investors Still Prefer Northern Virginia

JLL projected this year that Texas will surpass Virginia as the leading state for data center development by 2030 as AI mega-projects gravitate toward markets with more land and power capacity. Northern Virginia maintained a 0.5% data center vacancy rate at end-2025 after absorbing more than 1 gigawatt of capacity in 2025, and retains the highest rental rates of any data center market nationally, per CBRE. Speakers at Bisnow's national Data Center Investment Conference and Expo noted that Ashburn cannot compete with West Texas on megawatts but will maintain higher unit rents and lower vacancy, making it a superior investment on a per-unit basis. The Manhattan-sized AI campuses driving the current boom require power capacity and land that Northern Virginia fundamentally cannot provide.

BisNow · May 12, 2026

Who Has the Power? Industrial and Data Centers Battle for Gigawatts

Data center construction and the AI boom are creating a structural competition with industrial/logistics users for electrical grid capacity, with data centers projected to reach 12% of national electricity demand by 2028, up from just over 4% in 2023. CBRE research shows tech and data center occupiers accounted for the largest share of manufacturing leasing in Q1 2026, driving a significant jump in manufacturing leasing from the prior year. Companies supporting the data center supply chain — including prefabrication contractors, component manufacturers, and maintenance operators — are leasing industrial space at growing rates, which Prologis described as a new structural driver within logistics real estate demand. Columbus, Ohio recorded 13 million square feet of net industrial absorption in 2025, trailing only Dallas and Phoenix, driven by data center growth and a major chip fabrication project.
